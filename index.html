Benchmarks, Simulation, and Trust

A one-day AAAI-27 workshop on how to evaluate autonomous systems that generate hypotheses, run experiments, and produce scientific claims.

Why this workshop?

Science is being automated. The methods used to evaluate it have not kept pace.

Autonomous research systems can generate hypotheses, design experiments, run code, and draft papers. Yet the community lacks reliable benchmarks, verification protocols, and controlled environments for understanding what these systems can—and cannot—do.

This workshop brings together researchers in agentic AI, evaluation, simulation, AI safety, and domain science. We will examine the full research process: ideation, experimentation, writing, review, reproducibility, and human oversight.

Topics of interest

We welcome technical, empirical, methodological, and position papers, including work that critiques current systems and evaluation practice.

Evaluating AI Scientist systems

How capable are these systems, really?

  • End-to-end and stage-wise benchmarks
  • Cost, latency, and resource profiling
  • Cross-system comparison and interpretability

Verifying scientific results

Can we trust what they produce?

  • Reproducibility and experimental validation
  • Hallucination detection and fact-checking
  • Statistical, causal, and domain-expert review

Evaluation methodologies

Who, or what, should do the judging?

  • Reliable and calibrated LLM-as-a-judge
  • Multi-model consensus review
  • Human–AI review and shared quality metrics

Simulation and testbeds

Where can discovery be studied safely?

  • Simulated labs and clinical environments
  • Multi-agent role-play for expert review
  • Transfer from simulation to real practice

Also in scope: scientist–AI collaboration, oversight and trust, responsible deployment, and transfer from simulated to real scientific practice.

AI Scientist Evaluation Challenge

A shared task for building research systems—and the methods used to judge them.

Organized with XtalPi, the challenge will use a real-world molecular property prediction or drug–target interaction dataset. An expert-annotated corpus of AI-generated papers is planned for public release.

Dataset release date: to be announced.

Generation track

Produce a research report

Use an AI Scientist system to investigate the dataset. Reports will be judged by automated metrics and expert reviewers.

Evaluation track

Assess AI-generated papers

Build metrics, frameworks, or tools, evaluated for reliability, scalability, and correlation with expert human judgment.

Call for papers

Submissions should directly address the evaluation of autonomous scientific discovery.

Papers must use the official AAAI-27 two-column author kit and be submitted as an anonymized PDF. The submission portal will be linked here when available.

Full papers 6–8 pages

Original research on evaluation, verification, benchmarking, or simulation. References do not count toward the page limit.

Position papers and extended abstracts 2–4 pages

Perspectives, critiques, preliminary results, and emerging research directions.
