Why this workshop?

Science is being automated. The methods used to evaluate it have not kept pace.

Autonomous research systems can generate hypotheses, design experiments, run code, and draft papers. The community lacks reliable benchmarks, verification protocols, and agreed measures of what these systems can and cannot do, so genuine advances are hard to separate from findings that are plausible-sounding and incorrect.

Benchmarks for research agents have moved quickly. AstaBench spans more than 2,400 problems across literature work, coding, data analysis, and end-to-end discovery, and ranks 57 agents on a cost-normalized leaderboard. PaperBench scores replication against rubrics written with the original authors, while MLR-Bench and MLAgentBench score stages of the machine learning research cycle. Each of them measures a system.

Each of those measurements also comes from an instrument, and the accuracy of the instrument is established against human agreement. AstaBench scores end-to-end discovery with an LLM judge reading an auto-generated rubric, checks a judge against expert ranking on one of its ten subsets at a Kendall τ of 0.467, and finds that a system left out of the rubric's construction scores lower on identical work. Its authors record that last result as an open question. This workshop is where that question is asked, of benchmarks, rubrics, and judges alike, so that a reported gap between two systems can be attributed to the systems.

This workshop brings together researchers in LLM and agentic AI, benchmark and evaluation methodology, statistics, AI for Science across chemistry, biology, physics, and materials, the metascience and peer-review community, and industry practitioners in AI-driven discovery. We organize the program by the level at which the measurement question sits, so the three areas below partition the problem.

Topics of interest

We welcome technical, empirical, methodological, and position papers, including work that critiques current systems and evaluation practice.

What to measure

Which constructs, and against what ground truth?

  • Operational definitions of novelty, correctness, rigor, and significance, and their construct validity
  • Ground truth stronger than reviewer opinion, such as held-out empirical outcomes and laboratory confirmation
  • Process-level evaluation from agent execution traces

How to measure it

How is the instrument built?

  • End-to-end and stage-wise benchmarks for research agents
  • Contamination and leakage, and tasks that stay valid after training exposure
  • LLM-as-a-judge reliability, calibration, and adversarial robustness, with cost-normalized reporting

How good is the instrument

How would we know the measurement holds?

  • Reliability against validity of evaluation scores
  • Statistical power and minimum detectable effect
  • Sample-efficient evaluation through adaptive item selection, item response theory, best-arm identification, and off-policy reuse of prior judgments
  • Sensitivity of rankings to judge, prompt, and rubric

Also in scope, expert elicitation treated as an instrument with its own error model, reproducibility infrastructure such as execution traces, seeds, and cost logs, hazard and dual-use assessment for autonomous research in chemistry and biology, and Goodharting once a metric becomes a target.

Call for papers

Submissions should directly address the evaluation of autonomous scientific discovery.

Papers must use the official AAAI-27 two-column author kit and be submitted as an anonymized PDF through OpenReview. Submissions that compare systems must report at matched compute and token budget, or explain why matching is infeasible. Optional early submission opens ahead of the deadline, so reviewing proceeds on a rolling basis.

Full papers 6–8 pages

Original research on evaluation methodology, benchmarking, or verification. References do not count toward the page limit.

Position papers 2–4 pages

Perspectives, critiques, and emerging research directions.

Resource and artifact papers 2–4 pages

A released benchmark, harness, corpus, or tool, submitted together with the artifact. Reviewed on whether the artifact runs and is documented.

Reproduction and negative result reports 2–4 pages

Attempts to reproduce published AI Scientist results, including failures. No novelty bar, judged on rigor of reporting.
