Call for Papers

Evaluating AI Scientists: Benchmarks, Simulation, and Trust. A workshop at the 41st AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Montréal, February 22 or 23, 2027.

  1. Home
  2. Call for Papers

Overview

AI systems have gone from helping scientists with isolated tasks to attempting science itself. A growing family of autonomous research systems now generates hypotheses, designs and runs experiments, and writes up the results with little or no human involvement. What has not kept pace is our ability to judge this work. The first comparative benchmarks show that the strongest systems score poorly under automated review, that LLM reviewers barely agree with one another, and that coding agents fabricate or fail to validate experimental results in a large share of cases. Human peer review cannot absorb papers produced at machine speed, and we still lack controlled environments in which autonomous scientists can be watched, stress-tested, and compared.

This workshop brings together the communities that must jointly solve this problem, including researchers in agentic AI, evaluation methodology, simulation, AI safety, and the scientific domains themselves. The program features four invited keynotes, contributed paper and poster sessions, the AI Scientist Evaluation Challenge with a real-world drug discovery dataset, and a closing panel asking whether AI can truly judge AI.

Topics of Interest

We invite submissions on topics including, but not limited to, four themes.

1. Evaluating the systems. How capable are AI Scientists, really?

  • End-to-end and stage-wise benchmarks for autonomous research agents, spanning ideation, experimentation, and writing
  • Cost, latency, and resource profiling of autonomous research systems
  • Comparisons across systems and interpretability of AI-generated research decisions

2. Verifying the results. Can we trust what they produce?

  • Reproducibility of AI-generated experiments
  • Hallucination detection and fact-checking in scientific manuscripts
  • Statistical and causal validity of AI-proposed findings
  • Protocols for bringing domain experts into verification

3. Evaluation methodologies. Who, or what, should do the judging?

  • Reliability and calibration of LLM-as-a-judge approaches
  • Multi-model consensus review
  • Human-in-the-loop and human-AI collaborative review
  • Shared metrics for novelty, rigor, clarity, and significance

4. Simulation environments and testbeds. Where can autonomous discovery be studied safely?

  • Agentic simulations of laboratories, clinical settings, and other scientific environments
  • Multi-agent role-play as a stand-in for expert review
  • Transfer of findings from simulated settings to reality

We also welcome work on the human side of this transition, including how scientists collaborate with, oversee, and come to trust autonomous research systems, and what responsible deployment in real scientific practice will require. Position papers that critique current systems and evaluation practice are explicitly encouraged.

The AI Scientist Evaluation Challenge

The workshop hosts a challenge organized in collaboration with XtalPi, an AI-driven drug discovery company, built on a real-world dataset from molecular property prediction or drug-target interaction. Track A, generation, asks participants to use an AI Scientist system to produce a research report on the dataset, judged by automated metrics and expert reviewers. Track B, evaluation, asks participants to build metrics, frameworks, or tools that assess a provided set of AI-generated papers, judged on reliability, scalability, and correlation with expert human judgment. Challenge results will be presented at the workshop, and the annotated corpus will be publicly released as a community resource.

Details and registration

Submission Guidelines

We solicit two types of submissions.

  • Full papers of 6 to 8 pages, excluding references, presenting original research on evaluation, verification, benchmarking, or simulation of autonomous scientific discovery.
  • Position papers and extended abstracts of 2 to 4 pages, presenting perspectives, critiques, or preliminary results.

Format

Papers must be formatted in the AAAI-27 two-column style using the official author kit and submitted as PDF.

Submission site

The submission site will be announced here. Until then, questions about submissions can go to the workshop organizers.

Review

Reviewing is double-blind. Submissions must be anonymized, and each will receive at least two reviews from the program committee.

Publication

Accepted papers will be posted on the workshop website. The workshop is non-archival, so submission does not preclude publication of extended versions elsewhere, and we welcome relevant work under review at other venues where their policies permit.

Presentation

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the workshop and present in person, per AAAI policy.
